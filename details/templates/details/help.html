Help & documentation

How NExUS works

What each screen expects from you, what the statuses mean, and who to ask when something does not go to plan.

Getting in A proposal's life Documents & photos Your account If something is wrong

Getting in

  1. Register with your institutional email, campus, and — where you have one — your college and department. Your campus and department decide which proposals and reviews you can see, so pick them carefully; an admin can correct them later.
  2. Verify your email. The link in the message expires, and an unverified account cannot sign in. If it has expired, the sign-in page tells you so and you can request a new one.
  3. Sign in with either your username or your email. You land on the dashboard for your role.

Signed in and stepped away? Idle sessions end on their own and say so on the sign-in page, rather than dropping you on a blank screen.

A proposal's life

A proposal moves through these statuses in order. Most of the time the screen you are on names the next step; the tracker shows the same list as a progress rail.

Status What it means
Drafting Still yours to write. You and your named collaborators can edit it; nobody else can see it.
Submitted for Review Handed over. Editing locks at this point because reviewers are reading the version you submitted.
In Review Coordinators, the Director, and assigned evaluators are scoring and commenting.
Review Summary Issued The comments are compiled. Read the summary before the next status.
For Revision Returned to you with comments to answer. Editing reopens, and you resubmit when the comments are addressed.
Ready to Print Accepted. Generate the document in the required format and print it.
Submission & Uploading Submit the signed copies and upload the scanned set. Staff receive it from here.
Approved / Claiming Documents are released; the proponent claims them.
Proposal Completed Done. From here the work continues as a MOA and an implementation tracker.

After approval, two shorter trackers follow the same idea: the MOA tracker (or “not required”), then the implementation tracker, which ends with the terminal report and its photo documentation.

Documents & photos

  • The Programs page lists only what is actually behind it: approved proposals, plus programs entered by an admin as legacy uploads with their own supporting document. There is no manual “add a program” screen, so nothing on that page can drift away from a real record.
  • The Gallery shows photos uploaded with a terminal report's documentation appendix, each with the caption the proponent wrote. If no report has been filed yet, the gallery says so instead of showing placeholders.
  • Signed documents are uploaded by the proponent and released by staff; the proposal's document screen lists every version it holds.

Your account

  • A password needs at least eight characters, one number, and one uppercase letter. The field shows which rules are already met as you type, and the eye button beside it reveals what you have written and hides it again.
  • Forgot your password? The sign-in page has the link. The reset message goes to the address on your account; the form will not say whether an address is registered, which is deliberate.
  • Locked out after failed attempts? Sign-in pauses for a while after too many wrong passwords. The page says when that has happened and tells you to try again later.
  • Wrong campus or role? Those are set by an admin, not by you — ask the Extension Office to correct them.

If something is wrong

  1. Read the message at the top of the form. Errors are listed there first, in the same order as the fields, and each one is a link to the field it belongs to.
  2. If a page says it could not save, your entries stay in the form — nothing is lost by trying again.
  3. Still stuck, or something looks wrong? Send the page address and what you expected to happen to the Extension Office.
